# Meisou Life User Growth Map

**Version:** 1.0
**Purpose:** A humane path from first encounter to sustained participation.

## 1. Growth Philosophy

Growth at Meisou Life means growing a person's capacity for recovery, agency, belonging, and contribution. Business growth must arise from genuine value and earned trust, never from pressure, dependency, or attention extraction.

Growth is an ongoing practice, not a rank, score, spiritual status, or proof of awakening. Users must never be labeled higher, lower, advanced, awakened, or inferior according to their practice, beliefs, experiences, or time on the platform.

## 2. Two Growth Maps

### Internal philosophical growth map

`Pause → Awareness → Recovery → Zero Consciousness → Return to Origin (復本) → Yullyeo Alignment → Natural Intelligence → Consciousness Sovereignty (Brain Ownership) → Coexistence → Earth Citizenship → Earth Management → Coexistence Civilization`

This map guides curriculum design, product architecture, AI behavior, content development, community programs, and long-term strategy. It preserves the relationship between personal recovery, conscious self-direction, responsibility for life, and civilization-scale coexistence. It must not be exposed as a user ranking model.

### Simplified user-facing growth map

`멈춤 → 회복 → 리듬 → 주도권 → 공생 → 지구시민`

In English, this is: `Pause → Recovery → Rhythm → Self-Direction → Coexistence → Earth Citizenship`.

This map may appear in onboarding, dashboards, and general communication. It uses warm, everyday language and represents possible areas of practice, not levels a user must pass.

## 3. Journey Map

| Stage | User need | Meisou Life response | Healthy signal | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Discover | “Is this for me?” | Clear, calm explanation of the purpose and experience | Informed interest | Fear-based claims or urgency |
| First pause | “Can I begin now?” | A short, accessible free practice | A felt sense of ease or clarity | Complex onboarding |
| Return | “Can this fit my life?” | Gentle routines and user-controlled reminders | Voluntary repeat practice | Streak shame or excessive notifications |
| Belong | “Am I alone in this?” | Respectful community and shared practice | Safe participation | Forced social exposure |
| Deepen | “What support helps next?” | Transparent member experiences and choices | Informed membership decision | Hidden pricing or guilt-based conversion |
| Contribute | “How can I help?” | Invitations to share, host, or care for the field | Reciprocal contribution | Status competition |
| Renew | “Does this still serve me?” | Reflection, plan clarity, easy cancellation or change | Continued trust, including after leaving | Retention friction |

## 4. Entry Principles

- Make the first meaningful practice available with minimal friction.
- State what Meisou Life is and is not; it supports practice but is not clinical care.
- Use language that invites, not persuades through deficiency.
- Ensure price, membership scope, renewal, and cancellation information is understandable before purchase.
- Respect language preference and cultural context from the first screen.
- Begin with One-Minute Recovery rather than requiring advanced philosophical terms.
- Do not introduce Zero Consciousness, Return to Origin (復本), or Yullyeo as unexplained requirements at the product entrance.

## 5. Activation

Activation is not an account creation event. A user is activated when they experience a small, self-chosen moment of return and understand how to come back.

Recommended activation path:

1. Select language and current intention.
2. Take a short, optional grounding practice.
3. Receive a simple reflection and next-practice choice.
4. Offer an opt-in reminder with frequency control.
5. Present relevant community or membership pathways only after value is clear.

## 6. Retention With Integrity

Sustained use is earned through reliable benefit, not engineered compulsion. Use reminders sparingly, allow quiet periods, make pausing easy, and avoid loss-framed streaks. A user who leaves with a positive, autonomous experience remains part of the wider field of trust.

## 7. Membership Growth

Membership communication must answer four questions plainly:

1. What will I receive now?
2. What does it cost, and when will it renew?
3. How do I change or cancel?
4. Where can I get support?

The appropriate moment for an offer is after a user has had enough context to choose freely. Do not target emotionally vulnerable moments with heightened commercial pressure.

## 8. Community Growth

Community is grown through consistent norms, thoughtful facilitation, gratitude, and genuine opportunities to contribute. Encourage shared practice and care; discourage performance, spiritual hierarchy, and unsolicited advice.

## 9. Metrics

Use a balanced scorecard:

- Practice: voluntary return, completion comfort, user-reported usefulness.
- Trust: clear pricing comprehension, support resolution, cancellation experience.
- Belonging: safety, participation quality, report trends, reciprocal contribution.
- Sustainability: retention quality, referral based on genuine endorsement, operational viability.

Review metrics for unequal impact across languages, plans, and user groups. Do not optimize a metric that conflicts with recovery, agency, or dignity.

Never collect, infer, display, or optimize a spiritual-level score, enlightenment score, Zero Consciousness score, recovery leaderboard, or meditation leaderboard. Do not reward excessive platform use or punish absence through loss-framed streaks.

## 10. Growth Review Questions

Before launching a campaign, flow, or experiment, ask: Is the value real? Is the choice informed? Does the experience respect a depleted person? Can users leave easily? Would we be proud to explain the mechanism plainly?
